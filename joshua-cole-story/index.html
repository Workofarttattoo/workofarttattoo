My Story — Joshua Cole

Black-and-grey realism, vivid color tattoos, and a lifelong commitment to art, learning, and helping people feel welcome.

Hospitality, Artistic Training & Second Chances

We know what walking into a strange tattoo shop can feel like: the cool kids from school huddled at lunch, and you're the new kid looking for a seat — or even a friendly nod. At Work of Art, we lead with warmth that comes from a genuine heart of service.

I’m Joshua Cole. Before I built Work of Art, I worked in fine dining at Spago, Jonathan Bentley’s, Ruth’s Chris Steak House and The Shore Room. Those years taught me hospitality: listen closely, make people feel welcome, and treat every person with respect. That service mindset is still part of every appointment here.

My artistic education did not stop with a tattoo apprenticeship. I completed a full professional tattoo apprenticeship with Chris Oppenheim (@tattoosbychurch) at Interstate Ink. After my apprenticeship, I wanted to deepen my understanding of color and unlock what I could do with it as an artist. I enrolled in the atelier program at Evolve Artist Academy in New Jersey, where I studied color theory through hands-on exercises: mixing colors with one another, changing their proportions, and building tints and shades to understand how pigments interact. I learned which colors carry strong visual weight, which are more delicate, and how one pigment can change the appearance of another. I also trained to draw from observation—an essential skill I bring to accurate proportions in portraits and other custom tattoos. I later spent a year taking weekly private painting lessons with Sarah King of the King Art Institute in Las Vegas and attended 14 professional tattoo seminars led by accomplished artists. I’m currently enrolled in 10Masters’ advanced tattoo methodology courses in both vivid color and black-and-grey work.

Color has long been part of my tattoo practice, not a new direction. My work includes full-color sleeves, elaborate Superman and other Marvel-inspired character tattoos, vivid sunsets, galaxies and space scenes, watercolor effects, and custom color cover-ups. I love the way bold color, careful transitions, and light can bring an image to life. Color can also be one of the most effective tools in a cover-up: when the design, placement, and underlying tattoo allow it, thoughtful saturation, contrast, and composition can redirect the eye and transform old ink. Every cover-up is different, and I design around what the existing tattoo will realistically allow.

I keep practicing every day—oil painting, drawing, or teaching my current apprentice to develop her artistic and piercing skills. I also believe talent deserves a chance. Years ago, Quenton (@quenton_art) came to my shop after school as a 16-year-old aspiring artist, with his parents’ support. I mentored him for free before he was old enough to tattoo professionally. Our exchange was simple: he would help me learn photorealistic drawing, and I would share what I knew. Today he’s a respected traveling tattoo artist, earning $150 an hour and celebrating years of sobriety. Watching him succeed means more to me than any credential.

My own recovery has made service even more central to my life. Step Twelve teaches us to carry the message and practice these principles in everything we do. That is why I created Second Chance Ink and The 2% Club: one helps people move beyond old gang-related tattoos; the other honors hard-won sobriety anniversaries. I’m grateful for the people who helped me rebuild my life, and I want my work to help someone else move forward.

Don't just get a tattoo or piercing — leave feeling valued. Like you made a lifelong friend, or reconnected with an old one.
