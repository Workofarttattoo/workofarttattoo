The offering

A fresh start,
made by hand.

This is a personal commitment from Joshua Cole, owner and artist at Work of Art Tattoo & Piercing. Second Chance Ink offers donated cover-up work for people moving away from gang involvement and the tattoos tied to that chapter of their lives. Each project starts with a conversation about what is possible.

01 / THE DONATED WORK

Small tattoo.
Meaningful change.

Gang-related tattoos approximately palm-size or smaller may qualify for a complimentary cover-up. Joshua offers one or two donated appointments each month, fitted around regular clients. Availability is limited and scheduling is confirmed individually.

02 / LARGER PROJECTS

A contribution
toward your future.

For an accepted larger tattoo, Joshua donates the equivalent palm-size portion of the work. Any additional work is quoted separately and requires your agreement before proceeding. A larger project is not an entirely free tattoo.

03 / HONEST CONSULTATION

Real possibilities.
Clear expectations.

Placement, size, existing ink and the condition of the skin determine what can be done. Some tattoos need multiple sessions or additional treatment; some cannot be covered effectively. No result or complete concealment is guaranteed.

Why I’m giving back · Joshua’s story

Someone helped me.
Now it’s my turn.

I’ve lived through addiction, incarceration and difficult chapters of my life. Those experiences are part of my past. Today, I attend recovery meetings, work the steps and have an amazing sponsor who helps me become a better man every day.

My sponsor owns a sober living house. When I first got clean, he donated the bed I slept in. That act of generosity stayed with me. Someone helped me when I needed it, and I want to be that person for somebody else.

Having spent time in prison, I understand both sides of the fence. I’ve known people committed to gang life, and people who wanted a different future: to live, raise families, work honest jobs and become someone their children could be proud of. Those are the people I want to help.

“I can’t change someone’s past. But as an artist, I can help change what they carry into their future.”

I’ve already helped people with this kind of work, including covering a large black swastika with a cartoon bomb character. Second Chance Ink makes that commitment an ongoing part of Work of Art.

Las Vegas has given me an opportunity to rebuild my own life. This is one way I can give something meaningful back.

The details, with dignity

Clear limits.
No judgment.

A thoughtful cover-up starts with trust. Here is what to expect before you request an appointment.

You do not have to share your story publicly, appear in photographs or participate in a news feature to receive help.
Who is this for?

People leaving gang involvement behind who want help covering a gang-related tattoo. This is a focused community initiative, rather than a general free-tattoo offer. Eligibility and suitability are discussed privately, case by case. No criminal-history paperwork is required.

Do I need a referral?

Trusted referrals from recovery, reentry and community organizations are welcome and preferred, but you can also contact the studio directly. Ask for Second Chance Ink. You do not need to post your tattoo or personal history publicly.

What is free—and what is not?

Accepted, technically feasible cover-ups approximately palm-size or smaller are complimentary; no additional purchase is required for that donated work. For larger accepted projects, Joshua donates the equivalent palm-size portion and quotes the additional work separately. Any additional sessions or treatment needs are discussed during consultation before you commit.

Can any tattoo be covered?

No. Every tattoo needs an individual assessment. A new design may need to be larger or darker than the original. Some projects need multiple sessions or other treatment before a cover-up is feasible. A request does not guarantee acceptance, a particular design or complete concealment.

How soon can I get an appointment?

The commitment is one or two donated cover-ups per month, as time permits between regular clients. Consultation requests are welcome now; an appointment date is only confirmed after review and scheduling with Joshua. This is not a guaranteed walk-in service.

Will my identity, tattoo or story be public?

Participation does not require public identification or storytelling. Any photography, interview or use of your story requires separate, voluntary written media consent. You can decline publicity and still be considered. Consent can be withdrawn for future uses; material already published by others may not be retractable.

Do normal studio requirements still apply?

Yes. The studio’s usual age, identification, informed-consent, hygiene and appointment requirements apply to every participant. The consultation covers suitability, preparation and aftercare. See our studio rules for the standard requirements.

Three steps forward

Start with a conversation.

01

Reach out privately.

Call or email the studio and mention Second Chance Ink. Tell us how to contact you and the approximate size and placement of the tattoo. You can ask about the program before sharing sensitive details.

02

Make a realistic plan.

Joshua reviews the tattoo with you, explains the cover-up options and confirms eligibility, the donated scope and any additional quoted work.

03

Create something new.

If the project is accepted, agree on the design and an available appointment. Receive the same thoughtful artistry and aftercare guidance as any Work of Art client.
